# Content coverage report

Last updated: Phase 2. This is an honest status, not a claim of completeness. Syllabus references are to the Cambridge IGCSE Physics 0625 syllabus for 2026–2028.

## Physics 0625

**Structure:** 6 units, 26 lessons, 176 knowledge bytes (each with a test), 25+ original vector diagrams.
**Past-paper practice:** 1,885 multiple-choice questions (208 topical booklet questions and 1,678 Extended Paper 2 questions, 2020–2025) and 392 Extended Paper 4 written questions with mark schemes. Answers come from the official mark schemes. Topic grouping of the 2020–2025 questions is automatic and may contain mistakes.

### Still missing or thin

- Section 1 (measurement, motion, forces, energy, pressure) was reviewed earlier but not re-audited objective by objective in this pass.
- 2.1.2 supplement items on forces between particles and ions or electrons as particles are only touched on.
- 3.3 uses such as RFID and astronomy with radio waves, and the analogue versus digital explanation for sound, are only briefly covered.
- 4.2.1 experiments (charging by friction, detecting charge) are described only briefly.
- 4.5.1 experiment to demonstrate electromagnetic induction is not written up step by step.
- Explanations for the 1,885 multiple-choice past-paper questions are not yet written; learners see the official correct letter only.
- Diagrams are original code; none has been reviewed by a subject expert. Treat them as needing expert review before being relied on for teaching.

## Chemistry 0620

**Syllabus:** Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620, version for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028 (verified on the Cambridge International website). Content is original and was written from the published topic structure.
**Structure:** 6 units, 48 lessons, 149 knowledge bytes covering all 12 syllabus topics (particles, atoms and bonding; quantities and electrochemistry; energy and reactions; acids, salts and the Periodic Table; metals and the environment; organic chemistry and experimental techniques), each with one test, and 19 original vector diagrams (electron shells, dot-and-cross diagrams, giant structures, electrolysis, fuel cell, reaction profiles, rate graph, pH scale, reactivity series, blast furnace, organic formulae, fractional distillation, polymerisation, titration, chromatography, distillation).

Known limits:
- A syllabus audit was done against the objective list in the Cambridge syllabus PDF (Version 2). Gaps found were filled with 22 new bytes (gas volume laws, isotopes and chemical properties, three more electrolysis cases, evaluating rate methods, colour tests in redox, indicator colours and ammonium salts, hydrated salts, group number and ion charge, aluminium oxide layer, water treatment, pollution strategies and photosynthesis, ethanol uses, ethanoic acid reactions, PET and proteins, solution terms, locating agents, and anion and gas tests). The audit compared each objective with the lesson titles and by searching for key terms; it did not check each objective line by line against the wording of every question, so some Supplement detail may still be thin.
- There are no past-paper questions, mega-quiz extras or written exam practice for Chemistry yet. Test questions are original.
- Diagrams are original code and have been checked visually and for electron and atom counts where relevant, but have not been reviewed by a subject expert.
- No Core versus Supplement labelling yet.
- Calculation questions accept numeric answers only (no units in the answer box).

## Economics 0455

**Syllabus:** Cambridge IGCSE Economics 0455, for examination in 2027, 2028 and 2029. Content is original and was written from the published topic structure.
**Structure:** 6 units (basic economic problem; allocation of resources; microeconomic decision-makers; government and the macroeconomy; economic development; international trade), 36 lessons, 135 knowledge bytes each with one test, and 17 original exam-style diagrams (PPC and shifts, demand and supply curves and shifts, equilibrium, surplus and shortage, price changes, PED curves, maximum and minimum prices, indirect tax, subsidy, labour market, cost curves, exchange rate, business cycle). Diagram intersections are computed from exact formulae.

Known limits:
- A syllabus audit was done against the objective list in the Cambridge syllabus PDF (Version 1). About 45 new bytes were added for objectives the first pass missed: individual and market demand and supply, PES diagrams, social costs and benefits, the advantages and disadvantages of each government policy, forms of money, wage differences, mobility and division of labour, small and large firms, mergers, external economies, derived demand, capital and labour intensity, AFC, AVC, TR and AR, firm objectives, competition and monopoly, aim conflicts, the budget calculation, reasons for spending and taxation, policy effects on the aims, recession, the labour force survey, the CPI and its consequences, HDI strengths and weaknesses, poverty causes and policies, population rates and optimum population, trade restrictions and their reasons, MNCs, currency demand and the current account. The audit compared each objective with the lesson content and was done by reading the objective list, not by line-by-line comparison against every question.
- There are no past-paper questions or written exam practice for Economics yet. Test questions are original.
- Several topics (for example 2.9 market failure, 3.7 market structures, 4.2 to 4.4 policy, 5.x development) have no diagrams and would benefit from worked examples and data-response practice.
- Diagrams have been checked visually but have not been reviewed by a subject expert.

## English 0510 / 0511

Known limits:

## Question types and difficulty

Question types now in the app: multiple choice, select all that apply, matching, fill the gaps, true/false/depends, ordering, scenario, calculation, and written answers that learners self-mark against a mark scheme (definitions and exam-style questions). Past-paper questions are unchanged.

Multiple-choice questions in Business, Physics, Chemistry and Economics (written lesson tests only, not the past papers) were rewritten so that the options have the same length and style, and wrong options are common mistakes rather than obviously absurd statements. Measured by whether the correct answer is also the longest option: Business went from 91% to about 52%, Physics 61% to 34%, Chemistry 63% to 38%, Economics 65% to 51% (by chance alone it would be about 25 to 30%). A few questions still have a longer correct answer.

Known limits:
- Written answers are self-marked, so marking is only as honest as the learner. There is no automatic marking of free text.
- The new question types are used most in the end-of-lesson practice round and the Mega Quiz. Not every lesson has every type yet.
- The rewritten questions have not been reviewed by a subject teacher.
- English has no multiple-choice questions and was not changed.
